Sensor Inaccuracies & Physical Failure Modes

Physical environments introduce noise, occlusion, and surface artifacts that disrupt idealized mathematical sensor models. Understanding the domain-specific failure modes between ground platforms and aerial platforms is critical for selecting sensor packages.

🤖Ground Robotics Failure Modes

  • Narrow Beam Target Miss: A narrow 1° beam at long distance (>3m) can easily slip past small targets or rounded geometry, returning infinite distance (inf) or wall reflections behind the target.
  • Specular Reflection & Absorption: Dark matte materials absorb the infrared beam; glossy or glass surfaces cause specular reflection away from the sensor receiver.

🚁UAV-Specific Aerial Failures

  • High-Frequency Rotor Vibration: Unbalanced propellers induce angular jitter (Δθ). At 50m range, a minor 0.5° vibration causes 0.43m of point cloud positional error.
  • Oblique Incidence Angles: Drones pitch and roll to translate horizontally. Oblique ground strikes spread the beam footprint, reducing signal-to-noise ratio.
  • Prop-Wash Dust Artifacts: Rotor downwash near ground level kicks up particulate dust, generating false-positive obstacle detections.

Ground vs. Aerial Optical Disturbances

  • Rapid Exposure Dynamics: Transitioning from tree canopy shade into direct sunlight causes sensor saturation and temporarily blinds visual odometry.
  • Rolling Shutter Artifacts: Low-cost CMOS cameras reading pixel rows sequentially experience geometric image warping at speeds ≥5 m/s.
  • Scale Ambiguity: Monocular cameras cannot estimate absolute metric scale without baseline calibration or depth fusion.

Computational Overhead & Power Draw

Processing 1080p video streams at 30 FPS for visual SLAM or optical flow consumes substantial CPU/GPU wattage. On miniature quadcopters, onboard GPU power draw (10W-15W) can match or exceed the sensor's own power drain, placing double penalties on flight duration.

GNSS Multipathing & Signal Jumps

Near urban structures or dense foliage, GPS signals bounce off vertical concrete surfaces before reaching the receiver antenna. This multipathing phenomenon results in sudden position jumps of 2 to 10 meters, easily causing collision if directly fed into motion controllers.

Quadratic IMU Dead Reckoning Drift

Double-integrating uncalibrated MEMS accelerometer readings to compute position results in quadratic error propagation over time (E ∝ t2). Without visual or LiDAR velocity correction, dead reckoning drifts by tens of meters within minutes.

Power, Weight, and Mass Constraints

In ground robotics, adding a 1 kg sensor increases motor electrical draw marginally. In aerial robotics, payload mass penalizes total flight duration non-linearly due to aerodynamic hover thrust requirements.

The UAV Hover Momentum Relationship

Hover thrust required (T) equals total mass times gravity (m · g). According to aerodynamic momentum theory, required hover power scales to the 1.5 exponent of thrust:

Phover ∝ T1.5 ∝ (m · g)1.5

Worked Example: Adding a 500g mechanical LiDAR to a 2.0kg baseline quadcopter increases mass by 25% (1.25×). The resulting hover power draw increases by 1.251.5 ≈ 1.398×—a 40% increase in power consumption!

Classroom Case Studies & FSM Fault Architecture

Examine real-world robotic failures and test automated fault recovery state machines designed to prevent dangerous multirotor crashes during sensor degradation.

Case Study 1: Ground Robotics

Contaminated Barrel Cleanup

Problem: A ground robot attempting to attach to toxic barrels missed targets because a narrow 1° LiDAR beam returned inf at distances >3m.

Engineering Solution: Re-architected system roles. Used camera blob detection for mid-range tracking while restricting LiDAR to close-range (<1m) collision detection.

Case Study 2: Aerial Robotics

Powerline Inspection Drone

Problem: Solid-state LiDAR missed thin 1-2cm wire cables during rapid yawing, while stereo cameras failed due to sun glare.

Engineering Solution: Combined high-frame-rate solid-state LiDAR (100 Hz) with IMU-driven Kalman filter, and enforced a flight speed cap (v ≤ 2 m/s) to bound braking distance.

Seminar Discussion Prompts & Instructor Guide

Q1: Why might a $50 ultrasonic sensor be preferable to a $1,500 lightweight LiDAR for low-altitude UAV hold? ▼
Instructor Key Points: Ultrasonic rangefinders draw <0.5W, weigh under 30g, require minimal CPU overhead, and operate with high reliability over flat, non-absorbing surfaces like concrete or water where specular LiDAR beams scatter.
Q2: Why does sensor accuracy degrade in real hardware compared to simulation models? ▼
Instructor Key Points: Simulators primarily use simple Gaussian noise models. Real deployments encounter non-Gaussian environmental noise such as propeller downwash dust turbulence, direct camera sun glare, motor acoustic harmonics, thermal IMU drift, and non-Lambertian surface reflections.
